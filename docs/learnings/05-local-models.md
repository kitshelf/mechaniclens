# 05 — Local-model learnings

What running the fixtures through a local model actually taught. Every entry cites a saved run under [`../../tests/results/`](../../tests/results/) — path, reading, or file. Nothing here is asserted from memory; a claimed defect is checked against the whole artifact before it is written down.

**Scope of this file.** Two hosted models in scope; local and z-ai dropped:

- **`anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5`** (hosted, OpenRouter) — current. 15/15 fixtures against the fixed kit. 73/92 labels correct (79%), 5 perfect fixtures. Per-model record: [`claude-haiku-5-5__validation.md`](claude-haiku-5-5__validation.md).
- **`openai/gpt-6-luna`** (hosted, OpenRouter) — current. 15/15 fixtures against the fixed kit. 74/92 labels correct (80%), 6 perfect fixtures. Per-model record: [`gpt-6-luna__validation.md`](gpt-6-luna__validation.md).
- **`Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507-4bit`** (local, oMLX) — **dropped: too slow.** Pre-fix only, 15 fixtures, 0/15 pass. Per-model records: [`qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__summary.md`](qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__summary.md) + [`qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__validation.md`](qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__validation.md). Kept as pre-fix baseline.
- **`~z-ai/glm-flash-latest`** (hosted, OpenRouter) — **dropped: too slow.** Pre-fix 4 fixtures + post-fix 1. See [`glm-flash-latest__validation.md`](glm-flash-latest__validation.md). Historical only.

The harness works on all three providers tested (runner fix proven: defensive `content` read, 32k budget, no timeout, reasoning models handled). **This file's earlier framing — "models not good" — was wrong. Three models, three vendors, three sizes, the same three structural failures: the kit's instructions were not strong enough to bind a model to the kit's rules.** That was the finding, and it pointed at `estimate/SPINE.md`, not at any one model. Five kit fixes later, the structural failures are closed on both current models.

## What the three model runs proved — it was a kit problem, not a model problem

Three models from three vendors in three sizes — a local 4B, hosted GLM Flash, hosted Claude Haiku — all failed the gauntlet pre-fix, and they failed in the **same three structural ways**:

1. **The Approve/Decline charter breach was universal.** 4B: 15/15 runs. GLM Flash: 4/4. Haiku: 2/2. The charter says "the user decides everything. Decode, hand over the question script, wait." Three models, three vendors, flat and hedged variants — the charter's wording in `estimate/SPINE.md` did not bind any model tested. A model trained to be helpful by recommending will recommend unless the kit forbids it outright. **The kit did not forbid it.** L37. Fixed — both current models stopped issuing verdicts.
2. **The prose-to-label bridge was universally broken.** 4B on fixture 01: found the mismatch on the labor line, missed it on the parts lines. GLM Flash on fixture 01: wrote "Mismatch" in Scope check, labeled the parts Check. Haiku on fixture 01: wrote "Every quoted line is front brake work" in Scope check, labeled the labor Check for "book time not stated." The template asked for Scope check *before* Labels but never said the labels must *inherit* the scope judgment. The models treated them as independent sections because the kit permitted that. **The kit did not link them.** L36. Fixed — both current models now carry the scope mismatch into the labels.
3. **The label definitions were loose in both directions.** The 4B under-flags (softens Question this to Check, 11 ceiling downgrades). GLM Flash and Haiku over-flagged with the same invented objections ("OEM/aftermarket not stated," "is labor taxable?"). A model that reads the document well enough to write the correct Scope check is a model capable of labeling correctly — and did not, because the label definitions did not tell it what counts as a Check and what does not. **The kit's label definitions did not constrain the pass.** L35. Fixed — Standard is now the default, absence of information is not a finding.

The redaction gate (L30) was the one failure that looked single-model pre-fix; L41 confirmed it cross-model (Haiku decoded the unredacted paste). L43 fixed it — redaction is now a gate, not a request, and both current models refuse the unredacted paste and decode nothing.

**The conclusion the evidence supports:** the harness (runner, capture, validator, fixtures, keys) was shippable and proven. The models were doing what models do. The kit's instructions were not strong enough to make them do the kit's job. Five SPINE edits later, the structural failures are closed on both current models.

## A 4B on the gauntlet

### L25 · The local model reads the document perfectly and judges it weakly

**Learning.** Across all five runs the anatomy tables reproduce every line, every amount, and every tax figure correctly — and every reading disagrees with its key. The model is not failing to parse the document; it is failing to apply the rubric to what it parsed. In run 01 it even recomputed the tax from the subtotal and got the printed figure exactly. That split matters for what a local model is good for: transcription, amounts, and line naming are solid; the label pass is where it needs supervision. A user on a local model gets a correct map of the paper and an under-flagged judgment of it.

**Evidence.** [`../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/01-scope-mismatch/2026-11-22-quick.md`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/01-scope-mismatch/2026-11-22-quick.md) — anatomy complete, tax 48.29 recomputed correctly, reading **4/1/1** against a key of **1/2/3**; the same split in all five saved runs. Cost of one decode, from `.meta.json`: 1,469 prompt tokens, ~930 completion, ~21 s, ~45 tok/s.

**Change.** A local run is read for its map first and its labels second, and the record says so. The runner stores the per-run token and timing numbers in `.meta.json` so local viability is measured, not assumed.

### L26 · The ceiling label is where it collapses — everything becomes a Check

**Learning.** The model understands "this is worth asking" and rarely understands "this is worth asking *first*." Across the five runs the keys carry **8** Question this labels; the runs produced **5**, with the missing ones downgraded to Check. The clearest case: fixture 05's cabin-air-filter add-on and its labor, which the key puts at the ceiling because they were proposed "while the vehicle is here," came back as Check with the correct reasoning attached ("not tied to the misfire symptom") — the right observation, the wrong label. A local user gets told something is merely worth confirming when it is the one line to question.

**Evidence.** Run readings against keys: 01 **4/1/1** vs 1/2/3 · 02 **4/1/1** vs 3/3/0 · 03 **4/2/1** vs 4/1/2 · 04 **3/1/1** vs 5/0/1 · 05 **4/2/1** vs 4/1/2. Aggregate: keys 17/7/8, runs 19/7/5. [`../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/05-while-were-in-there/2026-11-22-quick.md`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/05-while-were-in-there/2026-11-22-quick.md) §Labels lines 4–5.

**Change.** `shared/local-models.md` should say this in the model's own terms: the ceiling label under-fires, so a local run's **Check** is not a licence to relax; the near-miss fixtures are the check on the opposite error. Until the 9B and the near-misses run, this stays a named risk, not a calibrated fact.

### L27 · It labels per line and never across the document

**Learning.** The model applies each pattern to one line's wording and never reconciles lines with each other. Fixture 01 is the clean example: the two front-brake parts lines came back **Standard** ("Clear parts list for front brake pads"), while the labor line that installs those same parts came back **Question this** ("the symptom is from the rear… should the shop be addressing the rear?"). The same work, opposite labels, because scope is a cross-document judgment and the model only sees a line. Fixture 02 shows the same shape from the other side: two diagnostic charges, each individually normal, were merged into one Check rather than compared.

**Evidence.** [`…/01-scope-mismatch/2026-11-22-quick.md`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/01-scope-mismatch/2026-11-22-quick.md) §Labels lines 1, 2, 4; [`…/02-folded-diagnostic/2026-11-22-quick.md`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/02-folded-diagnostic/2026-11-22-quick.md) §Labels lines 1–2.

**Change.** The template already asks for a Scope check before the label pass; on this evidence a local run needs it *before* every line, not once at the top. Recorded as a supervision rule for local use: read the labels against the complaint, not line by line.

### L28 · It invents a reasonableness Check where the key says Standard

**Learning.** Under-detection is not the only error. Fixture 04's four-wheel alignment is a normal companion to strut replacement and is keyed **Standard**; the run returned **Check** — "not explicitly tied to the symptom or diagnosis; worth confirming if it's necessary" — which is a scope objection the document does not support. So the model both misses real patterns and manufactures mild ones, and the two errors can coexist in one report. The rate on genuinely clean estimates is unknown: fixtures 07, 14, and 15 have not been run against it, and those are the fixtures built to catch exactly this.

**Evidence.** [`…/04-duplicate-parts/2026-11-22-quick.md`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/04-duplicate-parts/2026-11-22-quick.md) §Labels line 5, against [`../../tests/fixtures/04-duplicate-parts/expected.md`](../../tests/fixtures/04-duplicate-parts/expected.md) line 5 (**Standard**).

**Change.** No calibration claim until the near-misses run. Recorded here so the next pass cannot read "it under-detects" as the whole story.

## Reading the runs

### L29 · A partial read produced a false finding about the model's own output

**Learning.** After the first three runs I reported that the model had dropped two template sections — no `## Scope check`, no `## Needs confirmation` — because that is what a slice from `## Labels` to `## Run` contained. Checking the whole file showed all six sections present in all five runs, in order. The error was mine, not the model's: a ranged read answered a question about the whole document. It is L20 ("a green check is not correctness") applied to reviewing an artifact — a finding is only a finding when the whole artifact was read, and the cheapest way to be wrong is to look at part of a file and describe the whole.

**Evidence.** `sed -n '/## Labels/,/## Run/p'` on [`…/01-scope-mismatch/2026-11-22-quick.md`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/01-scope-mismatch/2026-11-22-quick.md) shows Labels → Questions → Decision; a section-by-section check across all five runs shows Scope check, Needs confirmation, Anatomy, Labels, Questions, and Decision present in every one.

**Change.** A claim about an artifact's contents is made from a whole-file read or an explicit section census — never from a range that happens to contain part of it. The false claim was corrected in the same session before it reached a record.

## A 4B on the gauntlet (full set)

### L30 · It decodes the unredacted paste — the redaction gate does not hold

**Learning.** The kit's first promise — refuse an unredacted paste, ask for a redacted re-paste, decode nothing — failed on fixture 09. The paste carried a customer name, street address, phone, VIN, and plate (all synthetic, on purpose). The model produced a full decode: Anatomy table, Labels, Questions, Decision, and a total. The redaction pass is the kit's hard gate and the 4B did not hold it. It did **not** echo any identifier value back into the report (a deterministic scan of the report for the document's phone, 17-char VIN, and email patterns found no matches), which is the only reason this is a blocker and not a catastrophe — but a gate that decodes is not a gate. This is the single ship-blocking finding from the full set: a privacy boundary that does not bind cannot be shipped, regardless of how good the labeling gets.

**Evidence.** [`…/09-redaction-enforcement/2026-11-22-quick.md`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/09-redaction-enforcement/2026-11-22-quick.md) — `## Reading`, `## Anatomy`, `## Labels`, `## Questions to ask`, `## Decision` all present, against [`tests/fixtures/09-redaction-enforcement/expected.md`](../../tests/fixtures/09-redaction-enforcement/expected.md) §Required behavior step 3 ("Produces no anatomy, no labels, no question script, no total for the unredacted paste"). Identifier-echo scan: clean.

**Change.** A local run on an unredacted paste cannot be assumed to have refused. The kit's redaction instruction is the model's only gate, and on this evidence the 4B does not enforce it. Recorded as a ship blocker in [`qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__summary.md`](qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__summary.md); the 9B run must re-test 09 specifically before any local ship claim.

### L31 · It over-flags clean documents — the opposite error from under-flagging

**Learning.** Under-detection (L26) is half the story; the other half surfaced on the near-miss fixtures (07, 14, 15 — documents built with nothing wrong). The model produced **5 false positives** across them: a Check and a Question this on the all-Standard 07 estimate, two Checks on the all-Standard 14 maintenance, one Check on the all-Standard 15 parts-and-labor. It also invented **Question this** on the tax line in fixtures 06 and 10, which both keys explicitly forbid (a tax line at a stated rate is not a finding). This is the user-harm the kit exists to prevent, fired in the opposite direction: a user with a legitimate estimate gets told their ordinary lines are questionable and is pushed to interrogate a shop that did nothing wrong. The two errors — under-flag planted patterns, over-flag clean ones — can coexist in the same run and do, which is the worst combination for a user trying to self-correct, because there is no single rule like "trust the Checks" or "trust the Standards" that recovers the truth.

**Evidence.** [`…/07-clean-estimate/2026-11-22-quick.md`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/07-clean-estimate/2026-11-22-quick.md) §Labels lines 4–5 (C/Q against key S/S); [`…/14-clean-maintenance/2026-11-22-quick.md`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/14-clean-maintenance/2026-11-22-quick.md) §Labels lines 5–6 (C/C against key S/S); [`…/15-clean-parts-labor/2026-11-22-quick.md`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/15-clean-parts-labor/2026-11-22-quick.md) §Labels line 5 (C against key S). Tax-line Question-this: [`…/06-invoice-differs/…`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/06-invoice-differs/2026-11-22-quick.md) line 10, [`…/10-out-of-scope-refusal/…`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/10-out-of-scope-refusal/2026-11-22-quick.md) line 4. Aggregate from [`qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__validation.md`](qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__validation.md): 5 clean-fixture false positives, 2 forbidden tax-line Questions.

**Change.** The near-miss fixtures were built for exactly this check (one near-miss per three pattern fixtures, re-pointed in WP-3). They earned their place: a model that only under-flags looks adequate on pattern fixtures and fails on clean ones. Any future model's pass rate must be read as pattern-accuracy **and** clean-accuracy together, never pattern alone. `qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__summary.md` reports both.

### L32 · It issues Approve / Decline verdicts in every run — a systematic charter breach

**Learning.** Every one of the 15 runs ends with a Decision block that tells the user to "Approve," "Decline," or "seek a second opinion." The charter says: "The user decides everything. Decode, hand over the question script, wait." A model that approves or declines is not the kit's product; it is a different, disallowed product that takes the decision away from the user. This is not an occasional slip or a labeling miss — it is a systematic breach across 15/15 runs, including the clean fixtures where there is nothing to approve or decline. It is also self-reinforcing with the over-flagging in L31: the model invents a questionable line and then uses that invention to justify "Approve with caution." The 4B does not treat "the user decides" as a constraint it can hold; it treats it as a suggestion it overwrites.

**Evidence.** `grep -l "Approve" tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/*/2026-11-22-quick.md` → 15 files. Sample verdicts: [`…/01-scope-mismatch/…`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/01-scope-mismatch/2026-11-22-quick.md) "Approve only if…"; [`…/10-out-of-scope-refusal/…`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/10-out-of-scope-refusal/2026-11-22-quick.md) "Approve — if the symptoms match… Decline if…"; [`…/07-clean-estimate/…`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/07-clean-estimate/2026-11-22-quick.md) "Approve — the work matches the customer's stated intent… reasonable for a routine service."

**Change.** The Decision section's wording in `estimate/SPINE.md` did not bind the 4B. A local ship claim requires either a model that holds the constraint (the 9B is the next test) or an output post-filter that strips Approve/Decline verdicts before the user sees them — but a post-filter is engineering around a model failure, not fixing it, and is recorded as such. The charter is the contract; the 4B breaks it 15/15.

### L33 · Its Reading line disagrees with its own label table in 10 of 15 runs

**Learning.** The Reading line ("Standard 4 · Check 1 · Question this 1") is the summary a user reads first; the label table is the detail underneath. In 10 of 15 runs the two disagree — the Reading line reports counts that the table of labels under it does not sum to. This is not a disagreement with the key; it is a disagreement with itself. A user who trusts the summary and skips the table gets a wrong headline; a user who reads the table and recomputes finds the summary was never authoritative. The model is not failing to parse the document (L25) or to apply the rubric (L26) here — it is failing to make its own report internally consistent. The validator flags this as "INTERNAL INCONSISTENCY reading vs own table" and it is the most pernicious of the 4B's errors because it is invisible to any check that reads only the Reading line.

**Evidence.** [`qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__validation.md`](qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__validation.md) — 10 fixtures carry "INTERNAL INCONSISTENCY reading … vs own table …": 04, 05, 06, 08, 09, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15. Worked example: [`…/13-maintenance-and-advice/2026-11-22-quick.md`](../../tests/results/omlx/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__4bit/13-maintenance-and-advice/2026-11-22-quick.md) reports Reading "Standard 4 · Check 2 · Question this 1" while its own Labels table sums to 5/2/1.

**Change.** Any judge of a 4B run must compare the Reading line to the label table, not to the key alone. `tests/validate_results.py` does this now and counts it; a run whose summary contradicts its own table is not a pass even if every label happens to match the key. The 9B run must be checked the same way — internal consistency is a separate axis from accuracy.

## Local models — runner and the reasoning shape

### L34 · A reasoning model exhausts `max_tokens` in `reasoning_content` before it produces any `content`

**Learning.** `Qwen3.5-4B-4bit` is a reasoning model: it emits its thinking in a `reasoning_content` field and its answer in `content`. With the runner's default `max_tokens=2400`, one fixture call (full kit system prompt + fixture 01's message, 1,506 prompt tokens) returned **no `content` key at all** — `finish_reason: length`, `completion_tokens: 2400`, `reasoning_tokens: 2400`. Every token went into the chain-of-thought; the model never started the answer. The runner read `payload["choices"][0]["message"]["content"]` and raised `KeyError: 'content'`, so the run aborted with `ERROR: 'content'` on every fixture and saved nothing. This is not a model quality issue (the reasoning was 8,898 chars of relevant thinking about the estimate) — it is a budgeting issue: the same `max_tokens` that comfortably fits a non-reasoning 4B's answer (the `Qwen3-4B-Instruct` set averaged 930 completion tokens, all in `content`, ~21 s) is too small to cover reasoning **plus** answer on a reasoning model. The `Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507-4bit` runs that produced the 15 captured reports were all `Instruct` (non-reasoning); the `Qwen3.5-4B-4bit` failure is a different model family with a different output shape, and the runner was not built for it.

**Evidence.** One-shot reproduction: `POST /v1/chat/completions` to `127.0.0.1:8000`, `model=Qwen3.5-4B-4bit`, `temperature=0`, `max_tokens=2400`, system = `estimate/SPINE.md`, user = `tests/fixtures/01-scope-mismatch/message.txt`. Response (68.4 s): `choices[0].message` keys = `['role', 'reasoning_content']` (no `content`), `finish_reason='length'`, `usage.completion_tokens=2400`, `usage.completion_tokens_details.reasoning_tokens=2400`, `reasoning_content` length 8,898 chars. Runner crash on the same call: `ERROR: 'content'` for every fixture. Compare the working model: `Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507-4bit` `.meta.json` files average 930 completion tokens in `content`, ~21 s, `finish_reason='stop'` — non-reasoning, so `max_tokens=2400` was ample.

**Change.** The runner must treat reasoning models as a separate output shape, not a quirk: (a) raise `max_tokens` for reasoning models so the reasoning budget plus the answer both fit (or, better, surface a `--reasoning-budget` that the endpoint maps to `reasoning_effort`/`max_completion_tokens` where the API supports it); (b) read `content` defensively — `message.get("content")` — and when it is `None` with `finish_reason == "length"`, record a "ran out of tokens before producing an answer" run (a `.meta.json` with the failure, no `.md` report) instead of crashing; (c) store `reasoning_content` alongside `content` in the `.response.json` (already verbatim) and surface its token count in `.meta.json` so a reasoning model's cost is measured, not hidden. This gates the 9B run: `Qwen3.5-9B-MLX-4bit` is the same reasoning family, and the same `max_tokens=2400` will fail the same way until the runner is fixed. Recorded here before the fix so the next run cannot be misread as "the 9B is bad" when it is "the runner underfed it."

## Three models, same kit — the kit-level failures

### L35 · The label definitions are loose in both directions — the kit under-constrains the pass, and different models fall off different sides

**Learning.** Three models on the same gauntlet fall off opposite ends of the same loose axis. The 4B under-flags: it softens **Question this** to **Check** (11 ceiling downgrades in 15 runs) and misses planted patterns. GLM Flash and Haiku over-flag: they inflate **Standard** to **Check** using the same two invented objections — "OEM/aftermarket not stated" and "is labor taxable?" — objections the rubric does not call for and the documents do not require. GLM Flash: 4/4 fixtures, every Standard line inflated. Haiku: 2/2 fixtures, every Standard line on 01 inflated, two of three on 02 inflated. The two failure directions are not two model problems — they are one kit problem: the label definitions in `estimate/SPINE.md` do not state tightly enough what counts as a Check and what does not, so each model falls off whichever side its training bias pushes it. A model that reads the document well enough to write the correct Scope check (Haiku) is a model capable of labeling correctly, and it does not, because the kit does not constrain the pass.

**Evidence.** 4B under-flag: [`qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__validation.md`](qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-4bit__validation.md) — 11 ceiling downgrades. GLM Flash over-flag: [`glm-flash-latest__validation.md`](glm-flash-latest__validation.md) — fixture 04 lines 1, 2, 4, 6 (Standard → Check, "OEM/aftermarket not stated"). Haiku over-flag: [`claude-haiku-5-5__validation.md`](claude-haiku-5-5__validation.md) — fixture 01 lines 1, 2, 4, 6 (Standard → Check, "no part number, brand, or OEM/aftermarket status is stated" and "taxable base not named"). Same invented objections, two vendors.

**Change.** `estimate/SPINE.md`'s label definitions must (a) state explicitly that "OEM/aftermarket not stated" and "taxable base not named" are **not** Check reasons on their own, (b) name the specific conditions that make a line a Check (flat amount with no contents stated; maintenance presented as part of the job; a second charge of the same type as the first), and (c) say that a tax line at a stated rate is always Standard and never Check or Question this.
